Welcome to the Teleprompter

  1. Get your script in. Paste it straight into the big black area, or use Import Google Doc in the side panel (it stays synced as you edit the doc).
  2. Press Space (or Play) to start scrolling. A 3-2-1 countdown runs, then read the line at the blue guide line.
  3. Adjust on the fly. ↑/↓ change speed, ←/→ jump back or forward, and the mouse wheel scrubs anywhere.
  4. Make it yours. The side panel sets your speed in words per minute, and its Display settings menu holds font, size, and colors — your choices are remembered. Drag its edge to resize it, or hide it with the » button.

Tip: italics in your script show up as highlighted riff sections, and [bracketed text] becomes a dimmed stage direction that isn't meant to be read aloud. Press ? any time for the full list of shortcuts.

Teleprompter — Instructions

What this app does

It scrolls your script smoothly up the screen at a speed you control, so you can keep your eyes up while you speak. Everything runs on your own computer: your script and settings are stored locally and are never uploaded anywhere. The only network access is fetching a Google Doc you explicitly link.

Getting your script in

  • Paste it. Click into the big prompter area and paste. Extra blank paragraphs are stripped automatically, and you can edit the text right in the prompter any time it's paused.
  • Import a Google Doc. Click Import Google Doc and paste the doc's URL. The doc must be shared as “Anyone with the link can view”. If the doc has several tabs, it reads the first tab and lists the others by name so you can switch with one click — hover a tab to preview its opening words. Re-open Import Google Doc to switch again later. (Paste a URL that already names a tab and it uses that one.) The prompter then stays linked: it re-checks the doc every 10 seconds and picks up your edits, and leaves the script alone when nothing has actually changed. Click the 🔗 badge to refresh immediately; right-click it to unlink.
  • Demo Script loads a sample so you can try everything out.

Script formatting

  • Italics become highlighted riff sections — talking points to improvise around rather than read word-for-word.
  • [Bracketed text] becomes a dimmed stage direction (e.g. [Pause for applause]) that isn't meant to be read aloud.
  • Images and charts in a Google Doc are imported and scaled to fit the column.
  • An imported doc keeps its structure: bold, underline, headings, bullets, numbering, indents, block quotes and tables all come across.

Playing and controlling

Press Space or the Play button. A 3-2-1 countdown runs, then the script scrolls at your chosen speed. Read the line crossing the blue guide line; text above it dims as already-read. While playing you can adjust speed with ↑/↓, jump with ←/→, or scrub anywhere with the mouse wheel. Pause to edit the text; Reset returns to the top.

The bottom bar shows the elapsed time since you started (in bold), the time left, and the estimated clock time you'll finish, based on the text remaining at your current speed. A thin progress bar runs along the bottom of the prompter.

Finding a spot

Click the magnifying glass in the bottom bar (or press Ctrl+F) and type. Every match lights up, the nearest one below the guide line is brought up to it, and Enter / Shift+Enter step through the rest. Handy for picking up from a particular line in rehearsal. Esc closes the box.

Display settings

Reading speed has a slider in the bottom bar next to opacity, and another in the side panel — they are the same setting, so either one moves both. The side panel also holds (under Display settings) font, text size, line spacing, column width, and text/background colors. The ☀️/🌙 button swaps between dark and light color schemes. Mirror flips the text horizontally for beam-splitter teleprompter rigs; Fullscreen hides everything but the script (controls reappear when you hover). Drag the panel's left edge to resize it, or hide it with the » button. In the desktop app, the Opacity slider makes the whole window see-through so it can float over other apps — it always stays on top.

FAQ

Why does my Google Doc need to be shared “Anyone with the link”?

The app reads the doc's web export directly, without signing in to your Google account. View-only link sharing is what makes that possible. Nothing is uploaded, and no one can edit your doc through the link.

My linked doc isn't updating.

Changes are picked up within about 10 seconds. Click the 🔗 badge to force a refresh. If it still doesn't update, check that link sharing is still on, or right-click the badge to unlink and import again.

Where are my script and settings stored?

Your display settings — font, size, spacing, width, colors, speed, opacity, mirror, and the side panel's width — are saved locally on your computer, along with the desktop window's size and position. Everything comes back exactly as you left it, even if the app is force-quit rather than closed. The script itself is not kept: paste it again, or re-import the Google Doc. Nothing is ever uploaded.

Does the elapsed timer stop when I pause?

No — it shows wall-clock time since you first pressed Play, including pauses, which is what matters for a live slot. Reset clears it. The finish estimate, by contrast, only looks forward: text remaining ÷ current speed.

How do I hit an exact running time?

Do one practice pass and watch the ends estimate in the bottom bar — it updates instantly when you change speed, so nudge ↑/↓ until the finish time matches your target.

What is Mirror for?

Professional teleprompter rigs bounce the screen off angled glass in front of the lens, which flips the image. Mirror pre-flips the text so it reads correctly in the reflection.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Everything works without a connection except Google Doc importing and syncing.
